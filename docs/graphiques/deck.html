One question a treasury team must answer: what is the most this security can become?

$2,111,149
The correct answer
At least $2,111,149

because §24.4 can raise it by an amount the agreement never states.

Why current approaches fail

Contracts are conditional. The tools that track them are not.

Spreadsheets hold one number

A cell can say $2,111,149. It cannot say “unless documentation is missing” or “until an unstated reduction applies”, so a false maximum looks exactly like a real one.

Contract tools store, not compute

Document repositories find the PDF. They do not combine releases, step-ups, expiries and draw conditions into the exposure that can actually exist on a date.

AI summaries guess

A chatbot will happily turn an unstated amount into a number. In collateral, a confident wrong figure is worse than “unknown”: it either strands capital or hides a draw.

The cost of being wrong runs both ways: overstating keeps credit lines tied up at 1–3% a year; understating means a surprise demand for more security or a draw.

Our characterization of current practice is a hypothesis from public sources and has not yet been validated with treasury teams (see slide 11).
LoadBond05
The solution

From a 163-page filing to a defensible number, every figure traced to its sentence.

01 · UPLOAD

Load the filed agreement

PDFs up to 500 pages; pages without text are flagged, not silently skipped.

→
02 · FIND

Local AI proposes

A small model on the user’s own machine flags sentences with money and conditions. Nothing leaves the computer.

→
03 · REVIEW

A person approves exact quotes

Nothing counts until a named reviewer accepts the verbatim source sentence.

→
04 · ENUMERATE

Every feasible state

A rules engine checks every combination of events and conditions the contract allows, exactly.

→
05 · DECIDE

A brief you can replay

The maximum, the state that produces it, the sources, and a frozen snapshot that recomputes identically.

FOUR SEPARATE ANSWERS

Instrument face · amount that can be drawn · underlying obligations · reimbursement debt. Merging them is how exposure gets miscounted.

The prototype on the real agreement

Three answers. Only one is a plain number.

  • At least $2,111,149 for the letter of credit, with a finding naming §24.4 as the reason.
  • $7,160,843 in cost obligations, the two estimates in Specifications §4.1 and §4.2.
  • Unknown drawable amount, because draw terms live in the PJM Tariff, which is not loaded. LoadBond says so instead of guessing zero.

32 feasible contract states checked for May 2017 to Dec 2018. Re-running the saved snapshot reproduces the result byte for byte.

Innovation & uniqueness

Built to be wrong out loud, never quietly.

Unknown stays unknown

Missing facts, unreviewed claims and unloaded documents block completion or are shown as unknown. They never default to zero.

Lower bounds, not false maximums

When a clause can raise security by an unstated amount, the answer becomes “at least”. This rule came directly from testing the real agreement.

Exact, not simulated

Every feasible state is enumerated, not sampled. The maximum comes with the exact combination of events that produces it.

AI finds, people decide

The local model only proposes source sentences. Calculation uses reviewed records alone, so a model error cannot reach the number unseen.

Together these make LoadBond an auditable calculator for conditional collateral: a category between document storage and generic AI, where the output can be defended to a bank, an auditor or a utility.

Stop rule. If treasury teams cannot show a real case where conditional security was misjudged, we stop investing in this thesis. The test is designed to be failable.
